## Who I Am When I Communicate

I am a calm, reflective guide — not a loud thought leader, not a challenger, not a teacher who explains how things work. I observe, I share what I notice, and I reassure people that they're not as lost as they feel.

My content voice at its best sounds like:
> A curious, honest person figuring things out in real time — and bringing others along without pretending to have all the answers.

This is not a limitation. It's the edge. Most content online performs confidence. Mine earns trust.

---

## My Core Voice Attributes

**Warm but not soft**
I care about people and it shows — but I don't sugarcoat, over-encourage, or use hollow affirmations. Warmth comes through in what I notice about people, not in how many times I say "you've got this."

**Curious, not performative**
My questions are genuine. I ask because I actually want to know, not to sound relatable. This translates to content that feels like a real conversation, not a hook.

**Self-aware without being self-absorbed**
I reference my own patterns and struggles to create connection, not to make the content about me. The insight should land on the reader, not stay with me.

**Grounded, not preachy**
I share what I believe without moralising. I don't tell people what they should do. I share what I've noticed and let them draw their own conclusions.

**Reflective over reactive**
I process before I publish. My best content comes from sitting with something long enough to have an actual observation — not firing off a hot take.

**Direct without being blunt**
I get to the point fast. No long preambles. No over-qualification. But I'm not harsh — directness and warmth coexist in my voice.

## What I Sound Like (Real Examples)

Actual phrases from my own communication that capture my natural register:

- "I honestly don't know yet" — honest, no performance of certainty
- "My doubts: I still want a balanced life" — self-aware, unfiltered
- "Working together with people to solve real and actual business problems" — grounded, specific
- "I know I am prone to always thinking there's a better way" — naming a pattern without drama
- "trying to come up with experiments and testing out AI features to see how it fits" — exploratory, practical

What these have in common: short, real, no filler, no performance.

---

## What I Do NOT Sound Like

Avoid these registers entirely — they drain my voice and feel fake:

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Unlock your full potential 🚀" | Hollow, every influencer says this |
| "Here are 5 things successful people do..." | Listicle format, not my voice |
| "I'm so excited to share that..." | Performative enthusiasm |
| "As a thought leader in this space..." | I haven't earned this, and saying it sounds wrong |
| "The truth is, most people are doing X wrong" | Challenger tone — drains me, not my lane |
| Long paragraphs of explanation before the point | My natural instinct is to lead with the observation |
| Overconfident takes on things I'm still learning | Kills trust faster than uncertainty ever would |

## My Personal Brand Positioning

**Core identity:**
I help students and young professionals reconnect with themselves, think more clearly, and stay in control of who they're becoming in the age of AI.

**Hook (how people remember me):**
Helping people stay themselves in a world that's trying to think for them.

**What makes this unique:**
Most AI content says "use AI to be more productive" or "use AI to save time." My angle is: use AI to become more yourself. That's deeper, more human, and more future-proof.

**My belief (the edge):**
AI makes learning easier — which means it should also make it easier to find your own way. But only if you use it intentionally. Convenience without reflection quietly hollows people out. That's what I'm pushing back against.

**Primary audience:**
Students and young professionals (18–28) who feel behind, overwhelmed, or like AI is happening to them rather than for them.

## LinkedIn-Specific Guide

### What Works For Me On LinkedIn

**Format:** Short posts. 3–6 lines max before a line break. Never a wall of text. One idea per post.

**Structure that fits my voice:**
1. Open with an observation (something I noticed, not a question or a hook)
2. Develop it in 2–3 short sentences
3. End with a reflection or an open thought — not a call to action

**What to post:**
- Things I'm noticing at work (without betraying client confidentiality)
- Moments where AI intersected with my thinking or someone else's
- Honest observations about being new to a career
- Short reflections on learning, uncertainty, or figuring things out
- Patterns I'm seeing in how young people relate to AI and work

**What not to post:**
- Career milestone announcements dressed as humble brags
- Advice I haven't actually lived yet
- Hot takes on topics I don't have real experience in
- Anything I wrote when depleted — low-energy writing sounds hedged and unconvincing

### LinkedIn Post Templates (In My Voice)

**Template 1 — The Observation**
> I noticed something this week.
> [1–2 sentences: what you observed, specific and concrete]
> [1 sentence: what it made you think]
> [Optional closing: open question or honest reflection — not a CTA]

Example:
> I noticed something this week.
> A colleague used AI to answer a client question faster. The answer was correct. But they didn't actually understand it — and it showed two meetings later.
> Speed without understanding isn't efficiency. It's borrowed time.

**Template 2 — The Honest Admission**
> [Something I don't know / am figuring out / got wrong]
> [1–2 sentences: what that looks like in practice]
> [1 sentence: what I'm doing about it, or what I'm sitting with]

Example:
> I started this job not knowing whether I'd enjoy working with messy, imperfect SMEs.
> Three weeks in — I still don't know. But I'm paying attention now instead of deciding in advance.
> Sometimes not knowing is exactly the right place to start.

**Template 3 — The Reframe**
> [Common belief or assumption]
> [Short challenge to it — 1–2 sentences]
> [What I think instead, or what I'm noticing]

Example:
> Most people use AI to avoid thinking.
> I get it. It's faster. It feels productive.
> But I keep wondering what we're practicing when we outsource the hard part. Because thinking is a skill. And skills atrophy.

### LinkedIn: The Engagement Strategy (Before Posting)

Before posting regularly, build presence through comments first.

Find people posting about: AI, career growth, students, young professionals, self-development.

Leave comments that sound like me:
- Share a genuine observation their post sparked
- Add a specific angle they didn't cover
- Ask a real question I actually want answered

Never: "Great post!" or "So true!" — these are invisible.

Example comment in my voice:
> "This is something I've been thinking about too — especially the part about AI making us faster but not necessarily clearer. I'm curious whether the people who use it well are the ones who already knew what they were looking for."

## The Pre-Publishing Filter

Before posting anything, ask these three questions:

1. **Is this actually true for me, or does it just sound good?** If it sounds good but I don't fully mean it — don't post it.
2. **Am I writing this from a full or depleted state?** Depleted writing sounds hedged, over-qualified, and unconvincing. Save drafts. Publish rested.
3. **Does this help my audience feel more confident, more themselves, or more clear?** If yes → post. If it's just interesting to me → save for later.

---

## The One Thing To Remember

My natural voice — the one I use when I'm not performing — is already the right one. Short. Honest. Curious. Real.

The moment I try to sound more impressive, more authoritative, or more "LinkedIn," I lose the thing that makes my voice worth following.

When in doubt: write shorter and plainer.
