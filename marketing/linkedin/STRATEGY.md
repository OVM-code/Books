# LinkedIn Strategy — Olivia Vanmalleghem

*The plan for what LinkedIn is for, what gets posted, how often, and how it connects to Substack and the books. Voice rules are NOT here — `voice/tone-of-voice.md` is the authority for how posts sound; this file only decides what gets posted and why.*

## What LinkedIn is for

LinkedIn is the discovery layer of a three-level funnel:

```
LinkedIn post (discovery) → Substack post (depth) → book (destination)
```

Every post stands alone and delivers its own value. A consistent fraction points readers to the Substack piece it came from, and the Substack pieces pull toward the books (`/book-to-blog` handles that link). LinkedIn's job is to put the observation in front of people, earn trust through honesty, and make the reader who wants more know where to find it.

Positioning (from `tone-of-voice.md`): helping people stay themselves in a world that's trying to think for them. Every post should be recognizably in service of that, whichever audience it addresses.

## Cadence

**2–3 posts per week.** Sustainable with a reflective, non-reactive style; enough presence to compound.

- One blog post yields 3 LinkedIn posts (see content mix), which covers roughly a week.
- Posts are written ahead into the queue and published rested — never drafted-and-posted in one depleted sitting. The queue exists so publishing day is a copy-paste decision, not a writing task (this operationalizes the pre-publishing filter's "publish rested" rule).
- Spacing: leave at least a day between posts derived from the same piece, and don't run all three in a row — interleave with native posts or engagement days so followers don't experience the same source material three days straight.

## Content mix

- **~2/3 derived posts** — generated from blog posts by the `/linkedin` skill: three per piece, one per template family (Observation / Honest Admission / Reframe), each built on a *different* beat of the piece.
- **~1/3 native posts** — things noticed at work, honest observations about learning and being new to a career, patterns in how young people relate to AI (the "what to post" list in `tone-of-voice.md`). These keep the feed from becoming purely a distribution channel — the account must read like a person, because it is one.
- **Engagement is content too.** On non-posting days, the comment strategy from `tone-of-voice.md` applies: genuine observations on others' posts in the AI / career growth / self-development space. Especially important while follower count is small — comments are how strangers first encounter the voice.

## Link policy (the funnel rule)

- Of the 3 posts derived from a piece, **exactly one is the funnel post** — it carries the link to the Substack piece. The `/linkedin` skill designates which (the one whose beat most naturally continues into the full piece).
- The link never arrives as "read my post" or any CTA register. The in-voice move is an honest continuation: *"I wrote about where this took me, here"* — the same rule as `/book-to-blog`'s pull toward the book: if it would sound at home under "buy now," it's off-voice.
- Link placement: in the post itself or first comment — decided per post at publish time, noted in the queue. (Reach mechanics on links change; the queue records what was tried so patterns become visible.)
- The other two posts end on reflection or an open thought, per the tone-of-voice structure. No link, no pointer. They build the trust the funnel post spends.

## Audience note

Both reader profiles are on LinkedIn: young professionals (18–28) and professional readers (AI/ERP/consulting). A post inherits its audience from the piece it derives from (check the project's `project.md`). Native posts may serve either. Don't blend registers within one post.

## The queue (`queue.md`)

Every generated or native post enters `queue.md` with a status:

- `drafted` — written, awaiting review
- `approved` — Olivia reviewed it, ready to publish
- `posted` — published, with date
- `dropped` — reviewed and rejected (keep the row; rejections are voice-calibration data — mine them for patterns per book-voice.md's growth rule)

## Review loop

Once a month or so, look back over `posted` rows: which posts drew genuine engagement (real comments, not vanity likes), which funnel posts actually moved readers to Substack. Fill in each row's `Resonated?` column in `queue.md` — that's what `/topic-radar` reads to weight future scans, so this step is what makes the feedback loop real rather than aspirational. Cadence and mix here are starting values, not fixed rules — update them as evidence accumulates.

## What never changes

The pre-publishing filter (`tone-of-voice.md`) applies to every post regardless of strategy pressure: true for her, written from a full state, leaves the audience more clear or more themselves. A gap in the queue is always better than a post that fails the filter.
